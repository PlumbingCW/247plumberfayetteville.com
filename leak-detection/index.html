Leak detection in Fayetteville, GA is the work of finding where water is escaping when you cannot see it. A hidden leak can soak a ceiling, a wall cavity, a crawlspace or the ground under your yard.

Our dispatch line is open 24/7. While you wait for the plumber, shut off the main valve if water is actively spilling, and move electronics and rugs away from the wet area.

Most hidden leaks give hints long before a puddle appears. A bill that keeps climbing, a meter that moves when nothing is running, a soft spot on a wall or a musty smell under the house all count. This page explains how to read those hints and how the search is done.

The meter keeps moving and the bill keeps growing

A water bill that creeps up with no change in how your household lives is the most common first clue. The cleanest check is the meter itself, usually in a box near the street or property line.

  1. 1

    Stop all use

    Turn off every faucet, the dishwasher and the washing machine. Make sure the ice maker is not filling.

  2. 2

    Read the meter

    Open the lid and look at the small leak indicator or the sweep hand. Note where it sits.

  3. 3

    Wait and compare

    Check again after about 30 minutes. Movement with everything off means water is leaving the system somewhere.

  4. 4

    Close the main and recheck

    Shut the main valve at the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps moving, the leak is on the line between the meter and the house.

That last step tells the technician which half of the property to search first. A silently running toilet causes the same pattern, so lift the tank lid and look before assuming the worst.

A stain, bubble or drip on a ceiling or wall

Water on drywall is already past the early stage, so stop the supply first. Close the main shutoff, then open a low faucet to drain the pipes. If a ceiling is sagging, stay out from under it. The 24/7 line is the right number when water is moving through a ceiling at night.

Not every stain is a plumbing leak. Roof leaks, air conditioner drain pans and sweating ducts look the same. Stains that grow only after rain point toward the roof. Stains that grow on a dry day point toward supply or drain pipes.

  • Ceiling stain under a bathroom: often a supply line, a drain joint or a shower pan.
  • Wall that feels cool and damp: a pinhole in a supply pipe behind the drywall.
  • Paint bubbling near the baseboard: a slow leak at a fitting or a tub valve.
  • Water only when a fixture is used: usually a drain or trap leak.

How a technician finds a leak without tearing things open

Good detection starts with questions and a pressure test, then moves to tools that confirm the spot. The aim is one small, accurate opening instead of a guess that costs half a wall.

Damp crawlspaces and the ground under the house

Leaks under a house go unnoticed for months because nobody looks there. Clues include a musty odor in the rooms above, soft or cold floors, and insulation that hangs wet. Standing water after a dry week usually means a pipe is leaking.

Piedmont clay holds moisture and drains slowly, so a small leak can keep soil soggy and hide where the water began. That is why a technician traces the pipes instead of following the wet ground. Do not crawl under a house where water pools near wiring. Shut off the main and let the plumber go in.

Wet spots in the yard and leaking irrigation lines

A lawn patch that stays soft in a dry spell, a low spot that fills with water or a sudden pressure dip can point to a buried line. If the line needs digging, call 811 first so buried utilities are marked. The repair itself falls under water service line repair.

Irrigation systems leak too. A zone that never dries, a weak sprinkler head or a valve box full of water are common signs. Turn the controller off and watch the meter. If it calms down, the sprinklers were the cause. Homes in 30214 and 30215 can have detection done for any of these yard problems.

A short note on pool and spa leaks

Pools lose water to evaporation, so a drop alone proves nothing. Mark the water level, run the pump normally and compare. If the level falls much faster than the weather explains, the leak may be in the skimmer or the plumbing lines to the equipment. Pressure testing can check those lines. Shell cracks are usually a job for a pool specialist.

Cost factors, insurance and the repair that follows

Price depends on how hard the leak is to reach and how many methods the search needs. A leak behind an access panel is a different job from one under finished tile. Pipe age, material and permit needs also matter. A good quote lists detection and repair separately. For a clear price on your own situation, call (770) 214-4545.

Homeowners policies generally respond to sudden accidental water damage. They usually do not cover wear and tear or the repair of the failed pipe itself, and policies differ, so check yours. Keep photos and water bills. If a pipe split open, see burst pipe repair. If the evidence points to the floor, slab leak repair covers the options.
