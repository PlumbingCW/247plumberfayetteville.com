Emergencies we handle in Peachtree City

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for the problems that cannot wait until morning. These are the usual ones for homes anywhere in Fayette County.

  • Burst or cracked pipes: a split supply line floods quickly, and burst pipe repair starts with the main shutoff.
  • Sewage backing up: water rising in a tub or floor drain points to a main line problem, so sewer line cleaning is the usual first step.
  • No hot water or a leaking tank: a pooling heater needs water heater repair or a swap, and it should be shut off at the supply.
  • Clogged drains and overflowing toilets: drain cleaning clears the line while you keep the water off.

Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak

A few minutes of work from you can save the floors. Do these in order and stop as soon as it is not safe.

  1. 1

    Shut off the main valve

    Turn it clockwise at the meter or where the line enters the house. Open a low faucet to drain the pipes.

  2. 2

    Kill power near the water

    Switch off breakers for wet rooms only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

  3. 3

    Clear the area

    Lift rugs and electronics off the floor and keep children and pets out of the wet space.

Sewer service here belongs to a different authority

According to Fayette County, the Peachtree City Water and Sewerage Authority is the sewer provider for Peachtree City, while Fayetteville has its own authority. That split matters at 3 a.m. because a backup can sit in your private line or in the public main.

A plumber settles it with a camera. A sewer camera inspection shows whether the blockage is on your side of the property line. If it is in the public main, the authority handles it, and you will want the video for your records.

Lakes, creeks and the wet-yard question

Lake Peachtree was created in 1957 when Flat Creek was dammed, Lake Kedron feeds it from the north and Lake McIntosh opened to the public in 2013. Ground near water is often damp, and that makes a real leak easy to dismiss.

A simple meter test separates the two. Turn off everything that uses water, note the meter reading and check it again after an hour. If the dial moved, you have a leak, and leak detection can find it without digging up the whole yard.
