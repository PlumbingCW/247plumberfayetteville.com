What is documented about the Town at Trilith

The community launched in 2016 as Pinewood Forest and was renamed the Town at Trilith in 2020, when the studio next door became Trilith Studios. Urbanize Atlanta described it in June 2021 as 235 acres on former wheat fields, with groundbreaking in 2017.

Homes range from small cottages to large estates. The town also has shops, restaurants, a K-12 school and a Piedmont wellness center. Atlanta magazine ranked it ninth on its April 2021 list of vibrant metro Atlanta city centers.

Newer homes still need plumbing help

A newer home can still have plumbing problems. Water heaters, fill valves, faucet cartridges and drains wear with use, and a construction flaw can show up after move-in. None of this is special to Trilith, so treat it as general guidance.

  • Water heater: find the install date on the label. The U.S. Department of Energy says storage tank heaters commonly last 10 to 15 years, so a newer unit is not a reason to skip water heater repair when it rumbles or leaks.
  • Hidden leaks: if the water meter keeps moving with every fixture off, a leak is likely. Leak detection can find it without opening walls everywhere.
  • Fixtures: a running toilet or a dripping faucet wastes water all day. Toilet repair is often a small job.
  • Drains: one slow drain usually means a local clog. Several fixtures backing up together point to a bigger line problem, which is where drain cleaning and a camera look help.

Check your warranty and HOA rules first

If your home is new, ask the builder or warranty provider whether repairs have to go through them. A repair by someone else may change what the warranty covers, so ask before you book.

Read your HOA documents too, since some communities set rules for exterior work and visible equipment. Neither point is a reason to ignore a leak. If water is spreading, shut the main valve first and sort out paperwork afterward.

Verify whoever comes to your door

Georgia lets you check any plumber on the Georgia Secretary of State Licensee Search. Ask anyone who visits your home for a license you can look up before work starts.

This is advice for every home in the state, not a claim about any one company. Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), applies to new homes and old ones alike, and 811 should be called before any digging.
