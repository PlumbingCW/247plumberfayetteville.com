Downtown Fayetteville is the oldest part of the city, and its buildings were put up over many decades. A plumbing problem there can involve very old work, recent changes or both. Call (770) 214-4545 to talk through a leak, a backup or a fixture problem.

This page sticks to what is documented about the area and gives general guidance for older and converted buildings.

A county seat laid out in 1823

Georgia established Fayetteville in 1823 as the Fayette County seat, and the 1825 courthouse in the town square is the oldest in Georgia. Railroad tracks arrived in 1887. Buildings from different eras sit side by side here, which is why plumbing assumptions are risky.

City Hall and City Center Park opened in 2021 on Stonewall Avenue West. A single block can hold very old and very recent work, so a plumber should look at the actual pipes before quoting a fix.

The Railroad Street Historic District

The Fayette County News reported in January 2026 that the Railroad Street Historic District was added to the National Register of Historic Places. It lies less than a mile northeast of the square and includes part of North Jeff Davis Drive, once called Railroad Avenue.

The district counts 23 contributing buildings and nine non-contributing ones. The houses include Georgian, New South and gabled-wing cottages, small houses and ranch houses. Many have been converted to professional offices.

Before you assume anything about an old building

Age alone does not tell you what is inside a wall. Buildings that were homes and became offices may have had restrooms or sinks added later. Facts beat guesses, so start with these steps.

  1. 1

    Find the shutoff

    Learn where the main valve is, and turn it clockwise if a pipe lets go.

  2. 2

    Run a meter check

    Turn every fixture off and watch the meter. Movement points to a leak that leak detection can locate.

  3. 3

    Look inside the drain

    A sewer camera inspection shows the real pipe condition and any roots or breaks.

  4. 4

    Ask about use

    A converted office with several restrooms has different needs than a house. See commercial plumbing.

Permits and who to ask

The city runs building permits and inspections through its Building and Inspections office. The city page does not spell out plumbing permit rules, so ask the Fayetteville building department before a water heater swap or a pipe reroute, especially in an older building.

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and applies to repairs and new work. Before anyone digs for a water or sewer line, call 811.

Where downtown sits

Downtown centers on the courthouse square, with City Hall on Stonewall Avenue West. The Railroad Street district runs northeast from there along North Jeff Davis Drive. Boundaries here are approximate, so use the city map for exact limits.
