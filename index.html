The first ten minutes of a plumbing emergency

What you do before a plumber arrives often decides how much damage a leak or backup causes. These four steps are safe for most homeowners and take a few minutes.

If you smell gas, leave the building first and call 911 or the gas utility. Plumbing can wait until the air is safe.

  1. 1

    Stop the water

    Turn the main shutoff valve clockwise. It is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house.

  2. 2

    Keep power away from water

    If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, switch off those breakers only when you can reach them while dry.

  3. 3

    Protect what you can

    Move rugs, electronics and boxes out of the wet area, then take photos for your insurance claim before cleanup.

  4. 4

    Call the 24/7 line

    Tell the dispatcher what you see and hear. They book a licensed plumber and tell you what to leave alone.

A county seat that kept growing

Fayetteville has been the Fayette County seat since 1823, and the courthouse square still anchors downtown. The bigger plumbing story is newer. Census counts climbed from 5,827 people in 1990 to 11,148 in 2000 and 15,945 in 2010, so much of the housing stock grew up in the 1990s and 2000s.

Homes from those decades are now at the age when original water heaters, toilet fill valves, faucet cartridges and supply lines commonly wear out. If yours are original, ask a plumber to look at them before one fails at 2 a.m.

Our office sits at 365 Fayette Place near Highway 85, so crews work the Banks Crossing, Fayette Pavilion and downtown areas along with the Highway 54 and Highway 92 corridors. Ask the dispatcher about same-day appointments when available.

What the local water means for your pipes

According to its 2025 water quality report, the City of Fayetteville Water Department draws about half its supply from three deep wells and gets the rest from the Fayette County Water System. The 2024 Fayette County Water System report lists its sources as Lake Kedron, Lake Peachtree, Lake Horton and Lake McIntosh.

Neither utility report publishes a hardness figure, and mineral buildup depends on the water at your own tap. Before you choose a heater or a flush schedule, test your tap or ask the utility for its annual water quality report. A plumber can then size the maintenance to what the water really does.
