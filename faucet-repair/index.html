Faucet repair in Fayetteville, GA covers more than a dripping spout. It includes shower valves, bathtub faucets, the small shutoff valves under sinks, and slow shower or tub drains.

If water is spraying from a supply valve or a handle will not turn off, take it seriously. Our line is open 24/7, and the fastest way to limit damage while you wait is to close the main shutoff.

Most faucet trouble starts small, with a drip, a stiff handle, or a thin stream. A plumber can usually find the failed part from its symptom, then repair it or say plainly when the fixture is the thing to replace.

Three safe checks before you pick up the phone

These checks are safe for most homeowners and take little time.

  • Find the shutoff. Look under the sink for the two oval or round supply valves. Turn each clockwise to stop water to that faucet. Tubs and showers rarely have their own, so use the main shutoff there.
  • Clean the aerator. If pressure at the spout is weak, unscrew the tip and rinse out grit. Mineral buildup often explains a thin or sideways stream.
  • Dry the area and watch. Wipe the cabinet floor, then check again after an hour. A damp spot that returns tells you whether the drip comes from the faucet, the supply line, or the drain.

Do not force a seized handle or a stuck valve. A snapped stem turns a repair into a rebuild.

A handle that will not shut off or a valve spraying water

A burst supply valve or a faucet that will not close is a true plumbing emergency. It can reach cabinets and flooring quickly. Call the dispatch line at any hour, including nights and weekends, and ask about same-day help if the leak is slow but steady.

Close the main water shutoff first, then open the lowest faucet in the house to drain pressure from the line. If water is near an outlet or a panel, stay away from it.

Valves fail for ordinary reasons. Rubber washers dry out and stems corrode. A valve that has not been turned in years is the likeliest to fail when someone finally tries it.

Kitchen and bathroom faucets: drips, squeaks and weak flow

A steady drip from the spout almost always means a worn cartridge, washer or seat. Water around the base of the faucet points to O-rings or a loose mounting nut.

Single-handle faucets use a cartridge you can replace without removing the fixture. Older two-handle models use washers and seats that wear against each other. The plumber may check both handles in one visit.

Under-sink leaks are tricky to trace. The drip may come from the faucet, a supply line, a compression fitting, or the drain trap above it. Dry everything, then press a paper towel to each joint and see which one wets first.

Shower valves, showerheads and bathtub spouts

When a shower will not shift between hot and cold, or the water temperature swings when someone flushes elsewhere, the mixing cartridge is usually worn. Replacing it usually means removing the trim, not opening the wall. A shower that drips after you turn it off may have a failing cartridge or a damaged seat.

A showerhead that sprays unevenly is often blocked by mineral scale, and soaking it in vinegar loosens most of it. A tub spout that leaks from the diverter, the pull-up knob, is a separate part and often a quick swap. Replacing the valve body itself means opening the wall, and the plumber should explain the access plan first.

Slow shower and tub drains

Hair and soap form a mat just below the drain opening. Lifting the cover and pulling out what you can reach clears many slow drains. A plastic drain strip is gentler on pipes than chemical cleaners.

If the tub still holds water after you clear the opening, the blockage is deeper in the trap or branch line. At that stage a cable machine is the right tool, and our drain cleaning service handles it.

If every drain in the house is slow, the issue may be the main line, and hydro jetting can clear a line that a standard cable only pokes through.

Mineral buildup and local water: what to check

Fayetteville gets its water from two sources. The city draws about half from three deep wells, and the rest comes from the Fayette County Water System.

The published reports give no hardness figure, so test your tap or ask the utility for its annual water quality report (a Consumer Confidence Report). If scale keeps clogging aerators, that result tells you whether a filter or softener is worth discussing.

What goes into the price of a faucet or valve repair

Price depends on the part, the access, and the time of day. A cartridge swap on a visible faucet is quick. A shower valve behind tile takes longer. After-hours calls differ from scheduled visits. Insurance note: homeowners policies generally respond to sudden accidental damage, like a burst supply line that floods a cabinet. They usually do not pay for wear and tear or for the repair of the failed faucet itself. Policies differ, so check yours.
