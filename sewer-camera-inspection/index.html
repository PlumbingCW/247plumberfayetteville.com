A sewer camera inspection in Fayetteville, GA sends a small waterproof camera through your drain line so a plumber can see the pipe from the inside, with a recording you can keep.

It answers a plain question: what is actually wrong, where is it, and how bad is it. Fayetteville 24/7 Plumbing schedules inspections for home purchases, repeat clogs, and slow drains, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

The camera does not fix anything. What it does is replace guessing with footage, so you only pay for the repair the pipe really needs.

Buying a house: look at the sewer line before closing

A standard home inspection does not see inside a buried sewer line. A problem there can mean a large repair bill, and it often surfaces after the move-in boxes are unpacked. A camera run during your due diligence window gives you a recording to use when you negotiate repairs or a credit, or to walk away.

Newer does not mean clear. Much of the housing around Fayetteville grew up in the 1990s and 2000s, so many lines are now decades old, and even recent builds can have a sag or a crushed section from construction. Ask the seller for access to the cleanout and arrange the visit early, before the inspection window closes.

How an inspection visit works

The technician opens a cleanout, or removes a toilet if there is none, and feeds a flexible cable with a lit camera head into the line. The image shows on a monitor as the camera moves. Some units send a locator signal, so the plumber can trace the exact spot above ground.

A heavily blocked line may need cleaning first, because the lens cannot see through sewage or a root mass. If that happens, the plumber will tell you why and may suggest sewer line cleaning before the run. You can watch the screen and ask questions as it goes.

Locating the line and pinpointing a problem

Many older owners do not know where their sewer line runs. A locator can trace it from above, which matters before any digging. Marking the depth and the distance from the house tells you what a repair will involve, whether that is a short trench or a long run under a driveway.

Call 811 before any excavation so buried utilities are marked. If the camera shows damage, permit rules vary, so ask the Fayetteville building department before pipe work starts. In ZIP codes 30214 and 30215, that conversation happens before a shovel touches the ground.

How findings are graded

A plain way to read the results

Minor means the pipe is sound and needs only cleaning or watching. Moderate means a defect exists, such as roots at a joint or a small crack, and a repair should be planned. Major means a collapse, a deep sag, or a broken length that needs excavation or replacement soon.

Ask the plumber to show you the clip behind each grade. A good grading names the distance from the cleanout and shows what the camera saw, so you can compare it with a second opinion.

If the footage shows a defect, sewer line repair options range from a short spot fix to full replacement, and the recording helps you compare quotes.

Getting ready for the visit

  • Find the cleanout: tell the plumber where it is, or say that you cannot find one.
  • Clear the way: move bikes, planters, and boxes from the access spot.
  • Share the history: mention repeat clogs, gurgling, or lawn soft spots.
  • Hold off on water use: avoid running laundry or long showers during the run.
  • Ask for the recording: request a copy of the video and a short written summary.

If you are selling, a clean report can help the listing. If you are staying, it gives you a baseline to compare against later.

Limits, price, and insurance

A camera sees what it can reach. It cannot see past a full blockage, a severe belly full of water, or a pipe too small for the head. It also does not test for leaks that only show under pressure. The plumber should say what the run did not cover.

Price depends on line length, access, whether the line needs cleaning first, whether tracing is included, and whether you want a written report.
