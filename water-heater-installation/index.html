Water heater installation in Fayetteville, GA means fitting a new heater where none stood before, or changing the type, size, or position of the one you have. This page walks through the decisions, the site checks, and what a finished install should include.

Decisions to settle before install day

Most installation trouble starts with a choice made too late. Settling a few things in advance lets the plumber bring the right unit, fittings, and venting on the first trip. If your current heater has already failed, the replacement page is the better starting point.

Gas appliances and the smell of gas

Safety comes before scheduling

If you ever notice a gas odor around an existing heater or a new gas connection, treat it as an emergency for the fire department, not for a plumber. Walk everyone outside, leave doors as they are, and phone 911 or your gas utility from a safe distance.

A plumber can inspect the appliance once the utility says the house is safe. We do not promise a response time for gas leaks, since the utility must act first.

Venting, gas line size, and combustion air

A new heater is only as safe as the pipes and vents behind it. Gas units need a vent that carries exhaust outdoors without backdrafting, a gas line sized for the unit's demand, and enough air for the burner. Tankless models often need more of all three than the tank they replace.

Relocating or converting a heater

Moving a heater from a closet to a garage frees living space, and switching from electric to gas can lower running costs where gas is available. Each move changes the pipework, and larger remodel work belongs with general plumbing installation. Supply lines, the vent, and the drain all have to follow the new position.

Going from a tank to a tankless unit is the most involved conversion. It can need a larger gas line, new venting, and a bigger electrical feed for the controls. The tankless page covers the unit itself, and the same visit can handle the supporting plumbing.

Heat pump tanks are a third route. They need floor space, air movement, and a condensate drain, and they are not a fit for every closet.

Expansion tanks, valves, and the finishing details

  • Expansion tank: absorbs the pressure rise when water heats in a closed system.
  • Relief valve and discharge pipe: opens if pressure or temperature climbs too high and sends water somewhere safe.
  • Shutoff valves: let you isolate the heater later without closing the whole house.
  • Dielectric or flexible connectors: protect the joint between dissimilar metals and ease future service.
  • Drain pan where needed: catches a slow leak before it reaches the floor.

These small parts decide how forgiving the install is years later. Ask to see them listed on the quote.

Permits, inspections, and verifying your plumber

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is based on the International Plumbing Code with Georgia amendments, and the installer is responsible for following it. Permit rules vary, so ask the Fayetteville building department before a water heater swap or any rerouting of pipes.

You can also verify any plumber yourself. The Georgia State Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, run through the Secretary of State, offers an online licensee search. Doing that before you sign takes a couple of minutes and tells you the person is licensed.

What changes the cost of an installation

Price follows the unit you pick, its size, the fuel, the amount of new venting or gas line, and the access to the install spot. Electrical work and code upgrades to old connections add to the total. Minerals in the water affect long-term upkeep, especially for tankless models, and the water varies. Test your tap, or ask the water utility for its annual water quality report.
