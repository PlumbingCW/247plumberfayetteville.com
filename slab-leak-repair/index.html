A slab leak is a break in a water line that runs under or inside the concrete floor of your home.

In Fayetteville, GA it often shows up as a warm patch on the floor or the sound of water with nothing running. The line is open 24/7. While you wait, close the main shutoff, stay off wet flooring near outlets, and do not run the water heater.

The pipe cannot be seen, so the first job is proving where it is and what failed. The second is choosing a fix that fits the house. This page walks through the signs, the three repair routes and the limits of each.

Warm floor spots, hissing and a meter that will not rest

Slab leaks are described in everyday terms before anyone uses the word. A spot on the tile that feels warm underfoot, carpet that is damp along one wall, a faint hiss in a quiet house or a water bill that jumped without explanation are the usual stories.

  • Warm floor area: a hot water line leaking into the concrete and heating it.
  • Damp carpet or lifting laminate: moisture working up from below.
  • Meter spinning with all taps off: water is leaving the system somewhere.
  • Low hot water pressure: a supply line losing flow before it reaches the fixtures.
  • Cracks in grout or baseboards that were not there last season: slab movement, sometimes from soaked soil.

None of these proves a slab leak alone. A technician confirms it with a pressure test and listening equipment before anyone talks about opening a floor.

Water coming up under the floorboards

Water rising through flooring or pooling at the edge of a room is an emergency, at any hour. Shut off the main valve first, then switch off the water heater so it does not run dry. Turn off power to the affected room at the breaker panel only if you can reach it without stepping in water.

Pull up rugs, lift furniture legs onto blocks and photograph the damage for your insurer. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and the 24/7 dispatch line will tell you what to do while you wait. A weekend slab leak is no less urgent than a weekday one, because water under a floor keeps working on the wood and subfloor.

Spot repair, reroute or repipe: three ways to fix a pipe under concrete

Slab leak repair options compared

Spot repair through the slabOne clear leak in a line that is otherwise soundBreaks into the floor, and other weak spots may fail later
Reroute through walls or atticLeaky lines that are hard to reach under flooringOpens walls and ceilings, but leaves the slab alone
Repipe the affected linesPipes with repeated failures or visible wearHighest scope, most lasting result

The right pick depends on what the leak tells you about the pipe. One pinhole in an otherwise healthy line often suits a spot repair. Several leaks in the same loop usually mean the material is wearing out, and a reroute or repiping avoids digging up the same floor twice.

Clay soil, newer slabs and what that means for your home

Fayetteville sits on Piedmont clay, which swells when wet and shrinks when dry. Slabs and the pipes under them can shift a little over the seasons.

Much of the local housing stock grew up in the 1990s and 2000s, so many homes are old enough for pipes to start wearing out. If your house is in that age bracket, ask the plumber about the condition of the other lines, not just the leaking one.

Plumbing under slabs for homes in 30214 and 30215 is the same work in either ZIP code. What changes is the age and material of the pipe, which the plumber identifies on site.

Honest limits of a slab leak repair

No repair removes the risk of a second leak from a pipe that is wearing out everywhere. A spot repair fixes one hole. If the surrounding copper is thin, another pinhole can follow in a few months. Tunneling under the slab keeps floors intact but is not always possible, and some floor finishes cannot be matched after cutting. A fair plumber will say so before starting.

Floor coverings, cabinets and moisture damage are separate from the plumbing. Ask who handles patching the concrete and restoring the finish, since that step is often not part of the plumbing quote.

Who pays: cost factors and insurance for slab leaks

Cost depends on the repair route, how deep and accessible the pipe is, the floor finish above it, and how much restoration follows. A quote should itemize detection, repair and any permit. Permit rules vary, so ask the Fayetteville building department before pipe rerouting. For pricing on your own floor plan, call (770) 214-4545.

Homeowners policies generally respond to sudden accidental damage, not wear and tear, and they often exclude the repair of the failed pipe itself. Some policies cover the cost of accessing the leak. Policies differ, so check yours and report the loss early.
