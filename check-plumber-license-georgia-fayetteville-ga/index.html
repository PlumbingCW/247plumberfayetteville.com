How to Check a Plumber's License in Fayetteville, GA

Written by the Fayetteville 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 23, 2026.

To check a plumber's license in Georgia, use the online Licensee Search on the Georgia Secretary of State website.

Plumbers are licensed by the Georgia State Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, part of the State Construction Industry Licensing Board, which is run through the Secretary of State. Ask the plumber for the name and license number, type them into the search, and confirm the record matches the person and company at your door in Fayetteville, GA.

This takes only a few minutes. The rest of this post covers what to ask, what to get in writing, when permits come up, and the warning signs that should make you stop.

Who actually licenses plumbers here

Plumbing in Georgia is licensed at the state level, not by each city. The Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers sits under the State Construction Industry Licensing Board. The Georgia Secretary of State runs the board and keeps the public records.

That means a plumber working in Fayetteville is checked against the same state database as one working anywhere else in Georgia. You do not need a separate city lookup to verify the plumber.

A five-minute lookup, step by step

  1. 1

    Get the details

    Ask for the full name on the license, the license number, and the company name before the work is scheduled.

  2. 2

    Open the Licensee Search

    Go to the Georgia Secretary of State site and find the online Licensee Search.

  3. 3

    Enter the name or number

    Try the license number first. If it does not return a record, try the person's name.

  4. 4

    Read the record

    Check that the license type fits plumbing and that the status shows the license in good standing.

  5. 5

    Match the person

    Ask the plumber who arrives to confirm the name on the record. A real number on a different person is a warning sign.

Keep a screenshot or printout. If a dispute comes up later, you will know exactly what you checked and when.

Questions to ask any plumber before the visit

You can ask these on the phone. A professional plumber expects them and answers without fuss.

  • What is the license holder's full name, and will that person do or supervise the work?
  • Is the company licensed and insured, and can you share proof of insurance on request?
  • Will the price be written down before work starts?
  • Does this job need a permit, and who pulls it?
  • Who will be on site, and are subcontractors involved?
  • What happens if the work uncovers a bigger problem?

Our dispatcher will walk through these with you if you call (770) 214-4545. We state that our plumbers are licensed and insured, and you are welcome to run the lookup on whoever arrives. No one should be offended by that.

Get the scope and price in writing

A license tells you who the plumber is. It does not tell you what you are buying. A written scope fills that gap and protects both sides.

The document should name the work, the parts or fixtures, what is and is not included, and the price. Clear price before work starts is the standard we hold ourselves to, and it should be yours too. If the plumber finds more work, the change should come as an updated written scope before anyone proceeds.

Be wary of a price that is only spoken and of any pressure to pay in full before work begins. Ask what happens if you change your mind about an add-on.

Big jobs and permits

Some work needs a permit and an inspection, such as a water heater swap or moving pipes. Rules vary, so ask the Fayetteville building department before the job. A licensed plumber should be able to tell you whether a permit applies and who handles it.

Jobs like water heater replacement or whole-house repiping are common examples where the question comes up. If a plumber tells you to skip a permit to save time, treat that as a reason to ask more questions.

If something does not check out

Do not let work start. Say thanks, decline politely, and find another plumber. If you already paid a deposit, gather the paperwork, photos, and messages in one place.

If you have a concern about a licensed plumber, start with the Secretary of State site and keep your facts simple and in order. Insurance questions are separate. Policies differ, so check yours before assuming any repair is covered.
