Hydro Jetting vs Snaking a Drain in Fayetteville, GA

Written by the Fayetteville 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 23, 2026.

Hydro jetting and snaking both clear drains, but they work in different ways. A snake, also called a cable or auger, pokes through a clog and pulls or breaks it up.

Hydro jetting blasts the inside of the pipe with high-pressure water and washes the walls clean. For a simple one-time clog in Fayetteville, GA, a snake is usually enough. For grease, roots, or clogs that keep coming back, jetting often works better.

The right choice depends on what is in the pipe and what shape the pipe is in. This post lays out both tools side by side so you can ask the plumber better questions.

The short comparison

How a snake clears a clog

A drain machine feeds a coiled steel cable into the line. The head spins as it advances. When it meets the clog, it either bores through it or catches it so the plumber can pull it out. Heads come in different shapes for hair, paper, or soft blockages.

Snaking is fast and does little to the surroundings. The weak point is that it opens a channel rather than cleaning the pipe. Grease on the walls stays. Over time the channel narrows again, and the symptom returns.

How hydro jetting works

Hydro jetting sends water through a hose and nozzle. Jets point backward to pull the hose forward and forward to break up the clog. The water scours the full circle of the pipe wall, flushes the debris to the main, and leaves the pipe much cleaner than a cable can.

The tool shines on thick grease, mineral scale, and fine roots. It is not a cure for a broken pipe, and the plumber should confirm the line is sound first. Our hydro jetting service starts with that check.

Which one fits your drain

Match the tool to the symptom. These rules of thumb help:

  • One slow sink, first time: snake it. A simple clog does not need a jet.
  • Same drain clogs every few weeks: jetting is worth a look, since buildup is likely on the walls.
  • Snake got stuck or only made a small hole: ask about jetting, once a camera has checked the pipe.
  • Kitchen line with heavy grease: jetting handles the coating better than a cable.
  • Roots in a sewer line: a cable can cut roots, while jetting clears the fine hair. A camera shows which is needed.
  • Old or cracked pipe: neither tool is a fix, and a repair plan may come first.

For a basic stoppage, our drain cleaning service covers both cabling and the camera work that tells you what is next.

Cautions for old or damaged pipe

Pressure is a strength and a risk. Old clay, thin cast iron, and pipe with cracked joints can break open under a hard jet. Water can also escape through a bad joint and soak the soil around it.

A camera look before jetting is the safe order of work. If it shows a crushed section or a bad offset, a plumber may suggest a repair first. The same caution applies to snaking, since a hard cable can snag on a broken edge. When the footage looks risky, the plumber should say so rather than push ahead.

What a homeowner can do first

Before you book either service, rule out the simple causes. Lift the stopper and clean it, try a plunger, and check whether only one fixture is slow. If several drains back up together, stop using water and call, because the problem is probably in the main line.

Keep grease, coffee grounds, and wipes out of the drain. Wipes marked as flushable still build up in a sewer line. Do not pour chemical cleaners in a drain that is completely blocked, since the plumber may need to work in that standing water.

Reading the quote

Compare quotes by scope. Ask which tool is planned, whether a camera is included, where the plumber will access the line, and what happens if the first tool fails. A jet job costs more than a cable job in most cases, so the plumber should explain why jetting is worth it for your pipe.

If you hear that one tool is always better, be careful. A good plumber picks the tool from what the pipe shows, not from habit.
