Water on the floor after hours: first moves for a business

A broken supply line, an overflowing restroom or a sewer backup in the middle of the night will not wait for opening hours. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows for problems that are stable. Commercial leak detection: what it finds and where it stops

Leak detection in a commercial building uses acoustic listening devices, thermal cameras, pressure tests and, in some cases, tracer gas. These methods narrow down a leak without tearing out walls, and they work best on pressurized supply lines. For more on the methods themselves, see our leak detection page.

The limits are real. A large building with many branches can hide several small leaks. Pump and HVAC noise can mask the sound of escaping water.

Leaks under thick slabs or in unmapped lines can take more than one visit to pin down. Detection tells you where to open up, not always what the repair will be. If a leak sits in a buried line under a slab, slab leak repair explains the options.

Grease trap cleaning: planned work that prevents emergencies

A grease trap catches fats, oils and food solids before they reach the sewer. When it fills, the trap stops working, odors build, and the drain behind it clogs. A backed-up kitchen floor drain on a busy night is usually the end result of a trap that was left too long.

Cleaning means pumping out the contents, scraping the walls and baffles, and checking that the inlet and outlet flow freely. Follow the manufacturer's guidance and any requirement from the local utility or your landlord on how often. Scheduling it ahead beats an emergency pump-out.

If the line beyond the trap is already coated, cleaning the trap alone will not stop repeat clogs. A jetting pass through the kitchen line can restore flow. See hydro jetting for how that works.

Backflow testing: a scheduled check on the water supply

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that keeps used water from flowing backward into the public supply. Restaurants, irrigation systems and fire sprinkler lines often have them. Testing confirms the internal checks still seal.

Whether a test is required, and how often, depends on the device and the water utility's rules. Ask the water utility serving your address, and keep the test report on file. This is planned work, not an emergency call. A device that fails its test usually needs rebuilt parts or replacement, and the plumber will tell you which before work begins.
