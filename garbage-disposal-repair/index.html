A garbage disposal is a small motor under a grinding plate, bolted to the sink and wired to a switch or outlet. It fails in a few predictable ways: it jams, the motor burns out, a seal leaks, or the drain behind it clogs. Each one has its own fix, and some are cheaper to replace than to repair.

Humming, clicking or silent: reading the sounds

A steady hum with no spinning means the grinding plate is stuck, usually on a bone, a fruit pit, or a piece of silverware. A click followed by nothing often means the same jam. A completely silent unit may have tripped its reset button, lost power, or burned out the motor.

First turn the power off, at the switch and at the breaker if you can. Never put a hand into the opening, even with the switch off.

Look underneath for a small hex socket in the center of the unit, and a red reset button near the base. Working a hex key back and forth frees many jams, and pressing reset after the unit cools restores the motor if it only overheated.

If the hex key turns freely but the unit still hums, or the motor is dead after a reset, the repair stops making sense. Replacing the motor rarely beats buying a new unit, so replacement is the common answer.

Dirty water under the sink or backing up from the dishwasher

Water pooling in the cabinet is the sign to act the same day. We dispatch around the clock, nights and weekends included, and same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows. Stop running the sink and the dishwasher until the leak source is found.

Disposals leak from three places. A leak at the top, where the unit meets the sink flange, means the mounting seal or the flange putty has failed. A leak from the side, at the dishwasher or drain-pipe connection, is often a loose clamp or cracked hose. A leak from the bottom or the body itself means the housing has rusted through, and that unit is done.

If food scraps and gray water rise in the sink when the dishwasher runs, the drain is clogged beyond the disposal. A cable machine reaches it, and our drain cleaning crews clear kitchen lines regularly. Grease is a common cause, so a line that clogs again within weeks may need hydro jetting.

The dishwasher hookup and the air gap

The dishwasher drains through a rubber hose that connects to a short nipple on the side of the disposal. On a new disposal, that nipple has a plug that must be knocked out first. Skipping that step makes the dishwasher fill the sink with dirty water.

A high loop in the hose, or a small chrome air gap on the sink deck, keeps drain water from flowing backward into the dishwasher.

If gurgling sounds come from the air gap, or the dishwasher smells after a cycle, the hose may be kinked or the connection partly blocked. Rules about how the dishwasher drain is routed vary, so ask the Fayetteville building department if you are changing the setup.

What should never go down the disposal

A disposal chops soft food into pieces that rinse away with running cold water. It is not a trash can, and some items do real harm.

  • Grease and cooking oil. They cool and coat the drain walls downstream.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells in quantity. They pack into a paste that traps other debris.
  • Fibrous food. Celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves wrap around the plate.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels. They swell into a starchy gum.
  • Bones, pits and non-food items. They jam the unit or damage the blades.

Run cold water for a short time before and after each use so scraps travel on down the line.
