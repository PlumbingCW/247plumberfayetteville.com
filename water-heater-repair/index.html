Same-day appointments are offered when available, and the plumber will tell you plainly if the unit is past saving.

Cold showers or water on the floor: do this first

No hot water and water coming from the tank both push a call to the top of the list. The leak matters more, because it can ruin flooring and drywall within hours. These steps are safe for any homeowner.

  • Cold supply valve: turn the handle on the pipe above the tank to the closed position to stop water feeding a leak.
  • Power or gas control: for an electric unit, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off, but only if you smell no gas.
  • Where the water comes from: note whether it drips from the top fittings, the relief valve pipe, or the base of the tank.

Water at the base often means the inner steel has corroded through. That is usually a replacement job, not a repair. Leaks at top fittings and valves can often be fixed on the spot. If this is happening after dark, call (770) 214-4545 and describe what you see.

Plenty of these faults come from one small part rather than a failed tank, which is why diagnosis comes before any quote.

Gas, electric, and heat pump units fail in different ways

Gas heaters rely on a flame, a vent, and a safety sensor. When one is dirty or worn, the burner shuts down. A weak thermocouple or a draft problem is a common reason the pilot keeps going out.

Electric tanks are simpler. Most have two heating elements and two thermostats. When only one element fails, you get short showers instead of none, which many people mistake for a small tank.

Hybrid or heat pump models add a compressor, a fan, and a condensate drain to the tank. They save energy, but a clogged air filter or a blocked drain can trip them into backup mode or a fault code. Keep the model label handy, since some repairs fall under manufacturer warranty terms. Tankless problems have their own page, covered under tankless unit service.

If you smell gas near the heater

What to do if gas is in the air: leave first, call second

A rotten-egg odor near a gas water heater is a safety matter before it is a plumbing matter. Do not flip switches, light anything, or look for the source. Get everyone outside, then call 911 or the gas utility from outside the building.

Call a plumber only after the gas utility or fire department has cleared the house. We make no promise about response time for gas leaks, because the utility has to deal with the gas first.

What a repair visit actually includes

The technician starts by checking the unit's age, model label, venting, and water and gas or electric connections. Then come tests of the element, thermostat, burner flame, relief valve, and tank pressure.

  2. 2

  3. 3

    Repair and retest

    The part is swapped, the unit is brought back up to temperature, and the relief valve and venting are checked.

A less common model may need a part ordered, and the plumber will say so honestly. The same visit covers homes in 30214 and 30215, the two Fayetteville ZIP codes this service reaches.

When fixing the old tank stops making sense

A repair is worth it when one part has failed on a tank that is otherwise sound. It stops being worth it when the tank itself is leaking, when the unit is near the end of its life, or when repairs keep stacking up. A leaking steel tank cannot be patched.

Much of Fayetteville's housing grew up in the 1990s and 2000s, so many original heaters are old enough that tanks tend to fail. If yours is one and the repair estimate is large, ask for the replacement figure side by side. The full picture of that decision lives on the replacement page.

What drives the cost of a heater repair

Price depends on which part failed, how hard it is to reach, and whether the unit sits in an attic, closet, or garage. Gas units may need venting or connection work that electric ones do not. A good quote lists the part, the labor, and any code-related items separately.

Insurance usually helps only when a sudden accident caused damage, such as a burst tank flooding a room. It generally does not pay for wear and tear or for the failed heater part itself, and policies differ, so read yours. For a quote on your unit, call (770) 214-4545.
